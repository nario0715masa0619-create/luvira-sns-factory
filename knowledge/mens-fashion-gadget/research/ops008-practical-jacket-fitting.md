## 2. Fact Check of Specific Claims

### 2.1 Shoulder width: "肩先とジャケットの肩線の位置"

**Claim**: The jacket shoulder seam should align with your natural shoulder point.

| Verdict | Reason |
|---------|--------|
| **B — Conditionally accurate** | For a standard business jacket, the shoulder seam should end near the shoulder bone. However, there is a small tolerance: some sources recommend about one index finger of room at the shoulder. Also, intentionally oversized or dropped-shoulder casual jackets are designed to break this rule. |

**Evidence**:
- Renoir: "the jacket shoulder should end where your shoulder does."
- Articles of Style: shoulder lines should correspond with natural shoulder width; no overhang, no sleeve protrusion.
- ONLY: "肩山と肩の先がフィットしているか" is the criterion; too tight restricts arm movement.
- Hanabishi: about one thumb-index pinch of room at the shoulder is acceptable.

**Safe phrasing for X**:
- 「肩の骨先に、ジャケットの肩の縫い目が近い位置に来るのが目安」
- 「肩パッドが大きく飛び出していないか、肩先で袖が無理に突っ張っていないか確認」

**Avoid**:
- 「肩先と肩線が完全に一致」
- 「1cmのズレは絶対にダメ」

---

### 2.2 X wrinkle: "ボタンを留めても胸にXジワが出ない"

**Claim**: If the jacket is the right size, no X-shaped wrinkles will appear at the button when fastened.

| Verdict | Reason |
|---------|--------|
| **B — Conditionally accurate** | The "no X wrinkle" rule is widely cited as a sign that the jacket is not too tight. However, it usually refers to the waist/torso area around the button, not strictly the chest. Horizontal pulling at the chest is a related but separate sign of a too-small chest. Also, aggressively slim cuts may show slight pulling even when technically "fitted." |

**Evidence**:
- Renoir: "The jacket closes without pulling or an 'X' wrinkle at the button."
- Butler Luxury: "If the suit is too tight, it will flair out at the top and bottom, and form an X-crease."
- Supremo: "細くし過ぎると、ボタンを締めたときに左右に引っ張られて×（バツ印の）シワが出ます。"
- Hanabishi: chest too small produces horizontal wrinkles; waist too tight produces pulling around the button.

**Safe phrasing for X**:
- 「ボタンを留めたとき、ボタン周りに引っ張られる感じや大きな×ジワが出ないのが目安」
- 「胸や胴回りに無理なシワができていないか確認」

**Avoid**:
- 「胸にXジワが出ない=サイズ完璧」
- 「Xジワが出たら全部間違い」

---

### 2.3 Sleeve length / shirt cuff: "袖からシャツが1cm前後見える"

**Claim**: About 1 cm of shirt cuff should show from under the jacket sleeve.

| Verdict | Reason |
|---------|--------|
| **B — Conditionally accurate** | 1 cm is a safe approximation for Japanese business style, but sources give ranges: 1–1.5 cm, 1–2 cm, or 0.5–0.75 inch (~1.3–1.9 cm). Western/casual styles may show more. The exact value depends on shirt cuff fit, arm length, and desired formality. |

**Evidence**:
- FLAWLESS: 1–1.5 cm ideal; Japanese style tends toward ~1 cm, Western style more.
- KASHIYAMA: 1–2 cm cuff exposure; shirt cuff should stop near wrist bone.
- UNIQLO: 1–1.5 cm shirt cuff visible.
- Articles of Style: 1/2"–3/4" (≈1.3–1.9 cm) of shirt cuff.
- Supremo: 1.0–1.5 cm.

**Safe phrasing for X**:
- 「ジャケットの袖から、シャツの袖が約1〜1.5cm見えるのが一般的な目安」
- 「1cm前後の見え方は、シャツ袖の長さやカフスのゆとりでも変わる」

**Avoid**:
- 「1cmピッタリが正解」
- 「1cm以上見えるとダサい」

## 3. Other Important Fitting Information

### 3.1 Chest ease

- Standard business jacket: chest circumference of garment ≈ body chest + 8–12 cm.
- Slim fit: +6–8 cm.
- Classic/relaxed fit: +12–16 cm.
- Practical check: when buttoned, you should be able to fit a fist between jacket and body without extreme pulling.

### 3.2 Jacket length

- Back panel should end just below the bottom of the seat.
- Casual jackets may be shorter; trend affects length.
- Sleeves should be trimmed but allow range of motion; sleeve head should be clean.

### 3.3 Waist / torso

- When buttoned, pulling the button away from the body should give roughly 2–3 inches (≈5–7.5 cm) of room for a trim fit.
- No extreme stress wrinkles.

### 3.4 Collar

- Jacket collar should sit cleanly against the shirt collar without gaping.
- About 1/2"–3/4" of shirt collar showing above the jacket back is ideal (business style).

## 4. Recommended In-Store Checkpoints (3–5 items)

Selected for: beginner-friendly, reproducible in a store, concrete, low risk of misunderstanding, and useful at a glance.

| # | Checkpoint | What to check | Why it matters | Source basis |
|---|------------|---------------|----------------|--------------|
| 1 | **Shoulder seam position** | The shoulder seam sits near the shoulder bone; no big pad overhang; the sleeve drops naturally. | Shoulders are the hardest part to alter. | FLAWLESS, Renoir, Articles of Style |
| 2 | **No pulling / X wrinkle at button** | After fastening the top button, the front does not pull strongly and no large ×-shaped crease appears around the button/waist. | Indicates the jacket is not too tight in the chest/torso. | Renoir, Supremo, Butler Luxury |
| 3 | **Shirt cuff exposure** | With a correctly sized shirt, about 1–1.5 cm of shirt cuff shows below the jacket sleeve when arms are relaxed. | A standard visual balance signal; too long looks sloppy, none looks heavy. | FLAWLESS, KASHIYAMA, UNIQLO, Articles of Style |
| 4 | **Collar lies flat** | The jacket collar hugs the shirt collar; no gap or roll at the back of the neck. | Sign of proper upper-back and neck fit. | Articles of Style, Permanent Style |
| 5 | **Length covers the seat** | The back hem covers the seat/bottom of the buttocks and hangs level. | Business-jacket convention; too short looks casual. | Articles of Style, Renoir, ONLY |

> These checkpoints are for standard business or smart-casual jackets. Intentionally oversized or fashion-forward cuts are exceptions.

## 5. Safe vs. Unsafe Expressions for an X Post

### Safe expressions

- 「目安」「一般的に」「個人差がある」「体感として」「この5点をチェックしてみる」
- 「約1〜1.5cm」「1cm前後」「大きなシワが出ないか」
- 「試着で確認できるポイント」
- 「ジャケット選びで気をつけていること」

### Unsafe / avoid expressions

- 「これが正解」「絶対にこれ」「40代はこれを着るべき」
- 「若く見える」「若返る」「女性はこう見ている」
- 「1cmピッタリが正解」
- 「このジャケットを買えばいい」
- Any claim that 40-year-old men have a different anatomical fit standard.
